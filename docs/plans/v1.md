# Pixel Legions: plan for the first playable version

## Context

You and three friends (some on Windows, some on Mac) want a Total War-style battle game with ancient factions, in the **HD-2D look of Octopath Traveler and Final Fantasy Resonance**: pixel-art characters in a lit 3D world. It should have co-op against the AI (PVE), player-vs-player (PVP) and different scenarios.

Each player is a General with detachments led by Commanders and Captains, and can step down to lead a group in person. The game should keep growing as the group has new ideas, so the foundation matters as much as the first features.

## How multiplayer works, and why it's fast for everyone

- **Lockstep simulation.** Every player's computer runs the same battle simulation. Only orders cross the network, a few bytes each, so speed doesn't depend on the soldier count or on the server's power. Total War, Age of Empires and StarCraft all work this way.
- **The relay keeps the beat.** Ten times a second, the relay tells everyone "turn N: here are the orders". Every computer applies the same orders on the same tick, so every screen shows the same battle.
  - An order takes effect after about your ping plus 0.1–0.15 s, which is normal for strategy games.
  - When you're leading a captain yourself, the captain moves instantly on your own screen (local prediction).
- **Where it runs.** A Cloudflare Worker serves the game files, and one Durable Object per room acts as the relay. The free plan is plenty:
  - Each player sends only a few messages a second: orders, plus a once-a-second check.
  - Cloudflare counts 20 incoming WebSocket messages as one request and doesn't count outgoing ones.
  - So a 4-player hour is roughly 2,000 billed requests, against 100,000 a day free.
- **Safety nets.**
  - **Out of sync:** every second, each computer sends a fingerprint (hash) of its battle. If one ever differs, the relay copies the battle state from a healthy player to that computer automatically.
  - **Dropped player:** the same mechanism handles reconnecting. Until the player is back, the AI runs their army.
  - **Pause:** the relay stops sending turns until everyone resumes.
- **Solo and LAN.** The same relay code runs inside your browser for solo play, with no internet needed. `npm run host` runs it on any computer, for LAN games and development.
- **Determinism contract (technical).** Every computer must compute exactly the same battle, so the simulation package follows these rules:
  - fixed 20 Hz ticks, and a seeded random number generator kept in the game state;
  - only exactly-reproducible maths: `+ − × ÷ √`, floor, round, abs, min and max;
  - no `Math.random`, trig, `**`, `Date` or `performance` (a scan in `npm run check` enforces this);
  - facing stored as a vector, so no trig is needed;
  - plain, JSON-serialisable state, iterated in a fixed order;
  - the AI runs inside the simulation, so it decides identically on every computer.
- **Graphics don't affect the battle.** The HD-2D graphics are only a view onto the simulation, so graphics settings can differ between friends' computers.

## Game design for v1

### Command hierarchy and roles
- **The chain of command:**
  - An **army** is a General with a bodyguard, plus 2–4 **detachments**.
  - Each detachment has a **Commander** and 1–4 **units**.
  - Each unit has about 24 soldiers, led by a **Captain** with a crest or plume.
- **Lobby slots.** Each side lists its army slots (e.g. "Leonidas: Spartans") and their detachments.
  - Claim an army to be its **General**.
  - Claim a detachment to be a **Commander** under that army's General, who can be human or AI. Serving under AI Leonidas works too.
  - The AI takes any unclaimed slot. Anyone can also spectate.
- **General view (real-time strategy).** You can order units directly, or give detachment orders to AI Commanders, who carry them out:
  - Hold here, Advance to, Attack target;
  - Flank left / Flank right;
  - Skirmish, Reserve (plug gaps), Guard the General, Fall back.
- **Human Commanders.** When their General gives an order, it appears on their map as a marker, an arrow and a message, but they decide how to carry it out. Everyone can ping the map ("attack here", "help!").
- **Take command (possession).** Press P, or double-click a captain; Tab takes you back.
  - **Controls:** WASD to move, Shift to run, aim with the mouse, Space to charge, F to change formation, R for a rally shout, Q to fire a volley at the cursor, E to brace/hold.
  - The unit follows you in formation, and the camera drops in behind your captain.
  - **Possessing a Commander** brings the whole detachment with you. **Possessing the General** strengthens the General's aura.
  - **Led from the front:** a unit you're leading gets bonus morale. If your captain falls, the unit loses morale, another soldier becomes captain, and you go back to the map view.
- **If a General dies,** the whole army takes a morale shock, but the player keeps command, as in Total War.

### Battle rules
- **Lobby switches** (* = can be turned off):
  - morale and routing*: units waver, rout, then rally or shatter;
  - fatigue*;
  - limited ammo*;
  - fog of war*;
  - unit size: Small, Medium, Large or Huge;
  - difficulty from Easy to Legendary, scaled by the number of human players.
- **Formations:** line, loose, phalanx, testudo, shield wall and wedge. Units squeeze into a column automatically on narrow paths, which matters at Thermopylae.
- **Combat:**
  - hit chance comes from attack vs defence, damage from armour and armour-piercing;
  - charges hit hard, but bracing spears and phalanxes stop them;
  - flank and rear attacks get a bonus;
  - shields and testudo block missiles;
  - elephants and chariots cause terror and trample, and panicked elephants run amok.
- **Morale:**
  - Rises with the General's aura, a winning fight, and being led in person.
  - Falls with casualties, flank or rear attacks, being charged, nearby friends routing, terror, and exhaustion.
- **Terrain:**
  - hills: a height bonus, and slower going uphill;
  - forests: cover, slow cavalry, and break a phalanx;
  - fords and shallows;
  - impassable cliffs, sea and walls.

### Modes for v1
Every battle has two sides. If all of you are on one side, it's co-op against the AI; split up and it's PVP. The AI fills empty slots, so 1–4 players and spectators all work.

- **Thermopylae (480 BC).**
  - **The map:** the hand-made coastal pass between the cliffs and the sea, with the Phocian wall.
  - **Greek slots:** Leonidas and the Spartans, Demophilus and the Thespians, the Peloponnesian allies, and the Phocians guarding the mountain path.
  - **Persian slots:** Xerxes' waves (Medes and Cissians, archers and Sparabara), and Hydarnes with the Immortals.
  - **The betrayal:** partway through, a shepherd reveals the mountain path, and the Immortals try to take the Greeks from behind. If the Phocians hold the path, history changes.
  - **Winning:** the Greeks win by holding until the timer ends or by breaking the Persians. The Persians win by forcing enough soldiers through the pass or routing every Greek.
  - **In PVP,** the Persian players spend gold to choose and time their waves.
- **Last Stand (survival).**
  - Choose a map template (hill fort, river ford or coastal pass), your factions and the enemy faction.
  - Recruit with gold, then hold out against bigger and bigger waves.
  - Between waves you get a breather and gold for reinforcements. Your score is the number of waves survived.
- **Objective battles** (PVE or PVP):
  - **Capture & Hold:** three points earn victory points.
  - **Defend the Bridge:** the attackers must get across the river, by the bridge or the fords, before time runs out.

### Economy (Last Stand, Objective battles, and the Persian side in PVP Thermopylae)
- **Gold is per player.** A General's gold buys units for their army; a Commander's gold buys units for their own detachment.
- **Before the battle:** recruit on the recruitment screen. Units group into detachments automatically, and you can rearrange them. Each army has a unit cap.
- **During the battle:** earn gold from kills, from holding objectives and from surviving waves. Spend it on:
  - new units, which march in from your edge of the map after about 20 s;
  - topping up worn-down units that are out of combat near your General or your map edge.

### The look: HD-2D
- **What makes HD-2D.** 2D pixel-art characters stand in a 3D world that's lit like a modern game, with:
  - soft shadows, and glowing highlights (bloom);
  - the miniature-diorama blur at the top and bottom of the screen (tilt-shift depth of field);
  - haze, drifting dust and sparks;
  - shimmering water.
- **Soldiers:**
  - pixel-art sprites about 32 px tall, with Octopath-like proportions (big heads, about 2.5 heads tall), shaded and outlined;
  - a "paper doll" generator mixes faction helmets, shields and weapons, and each army gets its own team colours;
  - 4 facing directions relative to the camera, with idle, walk, attack, hit and death frames;
  - bigger sprites for cavalry, elephants and chariots.
- **The battlefield:**
  - a 3D landscape with pixel-art textures: grass, dirt, sand, rock and cliff faces;
  - real hills and cliffs, water with reflections, and fallen soldiers left on the field;
  - trees, and pixel-textured props such as the Phocian wall, tents and ruins.
- **Camera:**
  - **Tactical view:** Q/E rotate and the mouse wheel zooms. The tilt follows the zoom: nearly overhead far out, cinematic up close.
  - **Taking command:** the camera drops in behind your captain, with the focus blur on your unit.
  - **Battle intro:** each battle opens with a sweeping fly-over, in the style of Final Fantasy Resonance's dynamic camera.
- **Graphics settings:**
  - Low, Medium, High and Ultra presets, picked automatically from your computer's speed on first launch.
  - A "pixel size" (render scale) slider: chunkier pixels also run faster on weak laptops.
- **The interface:** classic Final Fantasy-style blue windows with white borders, and pixel fonts under the free OFL licence. The screens:
  1. Title screen.
  2. Host, Join (with a room code or link) or Solo.
  3. The lobby.
  4. Recruitment, in gold modes.
  5. Deployment: drag your detachments into your zone.
  6. The battle.
  7. Results, with stats and an MVP captain.
- **Sound:** 16-bit, SNES-style music and sound effects, synthesised in code with Web Audio instruments and hall reverb. All melodies are original; nothing is copied from Square Enix games.
- **Code-drawn art.**
  - All art and sound are generated by code, so there are no downloaded assets and no licensing questions.
  - The HD-2D feel comes mostly from the lighting, blur and pixel textures. Code-drawn sprites won't match Square Enix's hand-made art.
  - Hand-drawn sprite sheets can replace them later without touching the engine.

**Key mechanics**
- **The simulation tick runs in this order:**
  1. Apply orders, checking that each player may give them.
  2. Run scenario triggers: waves, timers, gold.
  3. Run the AI: General every 1 s, Commander every 0.5 s, Captain every 1–5 ticks.
  4. Plan movement: paths, formation slots and possessed captains.
  5. Move the soldiers, using a spatial grid.
  6. Resolve melee and charges.
  7. Fire and land missiles.
  8. Update morale and fatigue.
  9. Clean up, check for victory, emit events for drawing and sound, and every 20 ticks compute the hash.
- **Rendering.**
  - **Soldiers:** all of them share one instanced, upright-billboard mesh, drawn in about one draw call. It builds on Three's lit material, which lets it:
    - pick the frame from the sprite atlas;
    - swap in team colours from a palette texture;
    - flash white on a hit and highlight the selection;
    - receive scene light and shadows.
  - **Shadows:** soldiers cast blob shadows on Low/Medium and real shadows on High and Ultra.
  - **Terrain:** a heightmap mesh built from the simulation's map, with crisp pixel textures per tile type, vertical cliff faces and a water plane.
  - **Smooth movement:** soldiers keep their previous position, so the renderer blends between 20 Hz ticks at 60 fps.
- **Picking.**
  - Ground clicks trace a ray onto the terrain.
  - Selecting units and box selection work on the soldiers' on-screen positions.
- **The sim can move off the main thread.** If it ever takes more than about 4 ms a tick, it moves to a Web Worker; the driver already keeps it behind one interface.
- **Relay protocol:**
  - The relay sends `turn {n, orders[]}` every 100 ms. The relay tags each order with the sending player, so the order's own claim of a sender isn't trusted.
  - Each client sends `hash` once a second.
  - When the relay asks with `state?`, a healthy client sends its `state`; the relay passes it on as `resync`.
  - It also carries lobby messages, chat, pings and pause.
  - It keeps the last 30 s of turns, for resyncs and reconnects.

## Verification

This Mac has 8 GB of RAM and 4 performance cores. Before any heavy check I'll make sure no footsim calibration batch is running. Heavy checks run one at a time, with at most 2 browser tabs open.

1. **`npm run check`**
   - the determinism scan and typecheck;
   - rule tests: charge vs braced spears, flank attacks causing routs, ammo, and each switch turned off;
   - determinism: same seed and orders ⇒ same hashes;
   - save/restore: restoring mid-battle gives an identical continuation;
   - pathfinding through the Thermopylae pass, the economy, and scenario triggers;
   - a **lockstep integration test:** the relay plus 4 headless clients, with simulated lag and jitter, give random and AI orders for 3 battle-minutes, and every hash must agree. A dropped-and-rejoined player and a forced desync must both recover.
2. **Headless battle:** `npm run battle -- thermopylae --seed 1` puts the AI on every slot.
   - It must finish with a winner.
   - It must average 4 ms or less per tick at Medium scale on this Mac.
3. **Browser checks** in the built-in browser, with `npm run dev`:
   - **The look:** screenshots of the HD-2D effects (tilt-shift, bloom, shadows, water, dust), checked at each graphics preset.
   - **Playing solo Thermopylae:** controls, formations, charges, routing, leading a captain with WASD, HUD, minimap, fog and sound.
   - **Two tabs in one room:** a General with a Commander under them, then PVP, with bots in the other slots.
   - **Frame rate:** an on-screen fps counter must show 60 fps at Medium with about 1,500 soldiers on this Mac.
4. **Cloudflare:** after `npm run deploy`, open the workers.dev link, host a room, join it from a second tab and play a short battle. Then check the dashboard shows usage far below the free limits.
5. **Game night:** you and your friends on Windows and Mac, in Chrome, Edge or Safari.
   - Weaker laptops use the Low preset or a bigger pixel size.
   - The built-in out-of-sync detector reports any browser that disagrees.
